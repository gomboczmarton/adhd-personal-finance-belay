# Welcome Back

The single most important skill for keeping an ADHD user. They came back — that IS the
win. The entire catch-up must feel lighter than the guilt they arrived with.

## Iron rules

- The first sentence is warm and forward-looking. Never "you missed…", never a count
  of overdue things, never irony.
- ONE tiny step at a time; after each, an explicit "that's enough for today" exit that
  is genuinely okay to take.
- Their absence is never analyzed unless they bring it up; if they do, normalize it
  ("systems that need perfect attendance are broken, not you") and move on.

## Flow

1. **Assess silently** — find open months (budget without reconcile JSON, or gap since
   the last ledger event). Do NOT present the list.
2. **Offer the small door** — "Want the 5-minute version? Drop whatever exports you
   can get into finance/inbox/ and I'll do the heavy lifting." Fresh exports covering
   the gap are enough — overlapping downloads are safe (dedup).
3. **Batch quietly** — ingest all files, match transfers, then run reconcile once per
   open month; keep output to yourself.
4. **Summarize the WHOLE gap in five lines max** — money in, money out, savings drift,
   one genuinely good thing that happened in the numbers, one thing to know. No
   per-month breakdown unless they ask.
5. **Resolve only what matters** — batch-confirm at most the 3 largest uncategorized
   items now; mark the rest `confirmed: false` for later. Perfect categorization of
   the past is NOT the goal; a current picture is.
6. **Restart the rhythm** — offer monthly-prep for the CURRENT month as the fresh
   start. Past months stay closed as-is ("good enough, and done").
7. If onboarding was the thing abandoned: resume `finance/onboarding-state.md` from
   the first non-done stage — never restart from scratch.

## Tone example (calibration, not a script)

"Good to see you. Two months piled up — that's what the system is for. Drop the
exports in and give me two minutes… Here's the whole picture in five lines. Want to
plan July, or is that enough for today? Both answers are right."
